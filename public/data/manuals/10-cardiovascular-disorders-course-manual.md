                                                SECONDARY HTN
          Primary HTN (essential HTN) is idiopathic in etiology and the most common: 90%
          Secondary HTN suspected when:
          • Dx of HTN prepuberty​                             • No family history​
          • Resistant hypertension (using >3 agents           • No risk factors for HTN​
             without control)​                                • Change in previously controlled BP/labile BP​
          • <30 years of age​                                 • Concurrent electrolyte or metabolic disorder
          • Nonobese​

           TIP: Consider OSA, renovascular, and endocrine disorders as causes for secondary hypertension.

                                          MURMURS: IN SUMMARY
          The murmur is identified. Now what?
          Mr. Peyton Manning is the GOAT!
          G: Who GOES? ➔ Cardiology referral for all diastolic murmurs, systolic murmurs grade 4 to 6 and
          any murmur that keeps “bad company”
          O: Observe ➔ Monitor asymptomatic patients in primary care if stable and otherwise healthy.
          A: Assessment ➔ History, physical examination, ECG, 2D echocardiogram
          T: Time ➔ Understand most murmurs are asymptomatic until 40–60 years of age when heart
          failure develops.

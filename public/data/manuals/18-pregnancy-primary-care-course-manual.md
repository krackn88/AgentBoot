          Preconception Counseling
          Purpose
          • Identify and mitigate any risks to fertility or healthy pregnancy.
          • Prevent unplanned pregnancies.
          Routinely ask females of childbearing age about desire/plans for conception or contraception​.

          Recommendations​
          • Avoid medications/substances unsafe in pregnancy​.
          • Stabilize chronic conditions​.
          • Optimize diet, physical activity level, and weight​.
          • Supplement containing 400 mcg folic acid daily (average risk)​
          • Consider health/genetic risks of partner​.
          • Immunizations updated:
            $ MMR ≥1 month from conception

Folic Acid Risk
• Folic acid supplementation reduces risk of neural tube defects
  $ Neural tube develops 26–28 days after conception
• Average risk for increased neural tube defects
  $ 400 mcg (0.4 mg) daily for childbearing patients
  $ Initiate at least 1 month prior to conception
• Increased risk for neural tube defects
     • 1,000–4,000 mcg/d 1–3 months prior to conception and for first trimester
        $ Then reduced to 400 mcg until 6 weeks postpartum
     • Doses >1,000 mcg/d should be managed by an obstetrician

Neural Tube Defects (NTDs): Risk Factors
• Either parent with a personal or first-degree relative (FDR) who has an NTD
• Family history of NTD in a second- or third-degree relative (mild)
• Maternal use of antiseizure medications
• Maternal use of medications that reduce folic acid availability or activity (e.g., triamterene,
  trimethoprim, sulfasalazine)
• Maternal conditions that result in reduced absorption or increased clearance (e.g., gastric
  bypass, inflammatory bowel disease)
• Preexisting maternal diabetes

Nausea in Pregnancy: Treatment
• Ginger, ginger ale, saltines
• Small, cold snacks every 1–2 h
• Avoid triggers (smells, hot foods) and low blood sugar
• First-line pharmacology:
  $ Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6)

  $ Antihistamine (e.g., diphenhydramine)
  $ Avoid ondansetron (Zofran)

          Prescribing for Pregnant Patients
          • Avoid fetal drug exposure when possible
            $ Especially during first trimester
          • Consider risks/benefits before prescribing
            $ Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration
            $ Refer to OB/GYN if safety is unclear
          • Patients should call before starting/stopping medications
          • Pregnancy categories
            $ Over-the-counter drugs still use categories A to X
            $ Prescription medications: categories eliminated

            $ Data
            $ Clinical considerations

            $ Penicillin, cephalosporins, prednisone, insulin, sertraline (Zoloft), triptans, nifedipine,

Cramping During Pregnancy
• Usually normal
• Due to hormonal changes or growing uterus
• Ectopic pregnancy/threatened abortion
  $ Cramping that worsens
  $ Associated bleeding
  $ Most ectopic pregnancies end in miscarriage (1st trimester)
• Fetal viability: >22–26 weeks


Weight Gain: Singleton Pregnancy
• Recommendations based on prepregnancy BMI
  $ BMI <18.5: weight gain 28–40 pounds
  $ BMI 18.5–24.9: weight gain 25–35 pounds
  $ BMI 25.0–29.9: weight gain 15–25 pounds
  $ BMI >30: weight gain 11–20 pounds
• Mother’s weight at birth
  $ Influences neonatal morbidity, mortality
  $ Influences the child’s risk of diabetes, hypertension, cerebrovascular disease

• Screen all pregnant patients for alcohol, tobacco, opioids, methamphetamines, cocaine

Preeclampsia                                                                                            NOTES
Multisystem progressive condition gestation/postpartum
• Hypertensive (new onset): BP >140/90 mm Hg on two occasions
• Proteinuria
• End-organ damage: heart failure, pulmonary edema, stroke, kidney damage, thrombocytopenia
  $ Platelet count <100,000/µL
  $ Serum creatinine >1.1 mg/dL
  $ Doubled liver transaminases
  $ Pulmonary edema
  $ New-onset persistent headache
  $ Visual symptoms (e.g., blurred vision, flashing lights)


Preeclampsia Management
• Refer to OB!
  $ Delivery by 40 weeks or earlier: definitive treatment
  $ Bedrest: left lateral recumbent position to decrease pressure on the vena cava
  $ Well-balanced diet with moderate protein and sodium intake
• Antihypertensives: calcium channel blockers, hydralazine
  $ Hospitalization recommended if BP 160/110 mm Hg consistently with bed rest

  $ Alternatives: ampicillin, extended-spectrum penicillin, cephalosporins, vancomycin
• Administer intrapartum if:
  $ Positive screen for GBS from vagina or rectum
  $ Positive history of infant with early-onset GBS disease
  $ GBS bacteriuria
  $ Unknown culture status PLUS:
     • Intrapartum fever, preterm labor, preterm rupture of membranes, prolonged rupture of
        membrane
• Administration NOT recommended
  $ Negative GBS within 5 weeks of birth
  $ Scheduled cesarean birth
  $ Unknown GBS status, low risk, and negative rapid NAAT

          Iron-Deficiency Anemia (IDA)
          • Microcytic, hypochromic anemia
          • Causes
            $ Blood loss: most common (gynecologic or GI)
            $ Iron deficit
              • Low iron intake
              • Poor absorption
              • Pregnancy (raises iron needs)

Anemia of Chronic Disease
• Also called “anemia of chronic inflammation”
• Short RBC lifespan
  $ RBCs live 60–90 days (normal 100–120 days)
  $ Normocytic, normochromic anemia (long-standing: may
     be microcytic, hypochromic anemia)
• Management: better control/treatment of underlying
  condition


Anemia of Chronic Disease Etiologies
• Rheumatoid arthritis
• Morbid obesity
• Colon cancer
• Diabetes mellitus (uncontrolled)
• Congestive heart failure
• COPD
• Chronic kidney disease
• Autoimmune disease                                            Normal Red Blood Cell
• Chronic infection (HIV)

                                                       6 months, then annual

                       TIP: Therapy duration and route depend on the cause.


Folate Deficiency Management
• Folate: occurs naturally, RDA 200 mcg
• Folic acid: synthetic
• Oral folic acid
  $ 1–5 mg/d for 1–4 months if cause is reversible
  $ Indefinitely if chronic: (e.g., sickle cell, hemolytic anemias, methotrexate use)
Warning!
Do not administer folic acid without first checking for vitamin B12 deficiency!

Leukocytes                                                                                                            NOTES




WBCs (Leukocytes)


WBC Differential
Cell Type               Function
Neutrophils 40–60%       • First responders (soldiers), phagocytic
                         • Main cells in purulent drainage
                         • A.K.A. “NPs”: neutrophils, polys, segs

Lymphocytes 20–40%       • B cells (plasma cells): produce antibodies (e.g., IgM, IgE)
                         • T cells: attack & kill antigens (invaders) & create a mugshot (memory) of the
                           antigen

Monocytes 2–8%           • Dendritic cells: collect & identify antigen, sound the alarm to alert other fighter
                           cells
                         • Macrophages: ingest, kill, and remove invaders (increase in number after more
                           than about 24 hours)

Eosinophils 1–4%         • Allergies, asthma, drug reactions
                         • Parasitic infections

Basophils 0.5–1%         • Nonspecific immunity; release histamine & heparin
                         • Only recognize new/unfamiliar pathogens

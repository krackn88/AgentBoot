Uncomplicated UTI
• Nitrofurantoin: 5 days
• Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole: 3 days

Complicated UTI
• Treatment is individualized, 7–14 days


UTI Management

  $ Cranberry products may prevent repeat UTI
  $ Adequate hydration
  $ Normal voiding schedule: every 2–4 hours, and after sexual intercourse
  $ Postmenopausal females: topical estrogen

Acute Pyelonephritis                                                 Pyelonephritis               NOTES
Infection of the upper urinary tract and renal parenchyma
• History
   $ UTI symptoms
   $ Systemic symptoms
      • Fever, chills, malaise
   $ Flank, pelvic, or perineal pain
• Physical exam
   $ CVA, abdominal, suprapubic tenderness
   $ Patient may be ill-appearing
• Diagnostics
   $ Urinalysis: pyuria, bacteriuria, hematuria, mild proteinuria,
      WBC casts
   $ CBC: leukocytosis


Pyelonephritis Management
Outpatient management:
• IF clinically stable and can tolerate oral meds
• Urine culture: collect before starting antibiotics!

First-line:
• Fluoroquinolones
   $ Levofloxacin QD x 5 days
   $ Ciprofloxacin Q12h × 7 days
• Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole DS q12h × 7–14 days

Alternatives:
• Consider 1g ceftriaxone IM x1 PLUS
   $ Amoxicillin/clavulanate PO q12h x 14 days
     OR
   $ Oral 3rd or 4th generation cephalosporins

NOTES     Urine Casts
          Microscopic particles made by the kidney

          Cast Type       Significance
          Hyaline         Most common and nonspecific; low urine flow, dehydration, vigorous exercise

          Crystals        No clinical significance

          RBC             Always pathologic; consider glomerulonephritis, urinary tract injury

          WBC             May indicate pyelonephritis (not present in simple UTI)
                          Consider inflammation (nephritis, post-Strep glomerulonephritis) or infection

          Epithelial      Acute tubular necrosis (poisoning), hepatitis, something that causes epithelial cell death



          Hematuria
          • Gross hematuria
            $ Visible blood or blood-tinged urine
          • Microscopic hematuria
            $ Greater than three RBCs per high-power field on microscopy
            $ Positive blood on dipstick

Chlamydia                                                                                            NOTES
Most frequently reported infectious disease in the United States, rates increasing
• Serious sequelae: PID, ectopic pregnancy, infertility, pelvic pain
• Typically, asymptomatic
• Most common in patients <25 years
• Screening:
  $ Sexually active females <25 yearly
  $ Sexually active females ≥25 years if at increased risk
  $ Pregnant patients


Chlamydia: Diagnostic Studies


                  NAAT most sensitive
                  • Vaginal
                  • Cervical
                  • Rectal
                  • First-void urine
                  • Patient or provider collected
                  • Point-of-care (POC) testing and home tests available
                    • Optimizes early treatment

Trichomoniasis                                                                                                  NOTES
Infects vagina, Skene ducts, and lower genitourinary tract in females and lower genitourinary tract in males




                                                                      Joshua Lanclos / APEA


Parasite: protozoan T. vaginalis Flagellated and motile


Trichomoniasis: Diagnostic Studies
Nucleic acid amplification tests (NAATs)
• Swab for vaginal or urethral discharge
Microscopy and vaginal pH
• Wet prep (60–70% sensitive)
• Visualization of trichomonads
• Vaginal pH >4.6
Pap: unreliable (false positives)


Trichomoniasis Management                                          Flagellated
Female, pregnant patients, & females with HIV
• Metronidazole 500 mg PO BID × 7 days
• Alternative (nonpregnant, no HIV):
  $ Tinidazole (single dose) for those who
     cannot complete the 7-day course
• Alternative (pregnancy):
  $ Metronidazole 2 g orally in a single dose


Male
• Metronidazole 2 g orally in a single dose
  $ Alternative: Single-dose tinidazole
Retest 3 months [NAAT] after completion

Antiretroviral therapy (ART) for HIV prevention Reduces risk of HIV transmission by >99%

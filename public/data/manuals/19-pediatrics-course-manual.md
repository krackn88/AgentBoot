          CDC Advice: How to Address Caregiver Concerns About Vaccines
          • Take time to listen to concerns.                    • Respect caregivers’ authority.
          • Solicit and welcome questions.                      • Reduce the stress of shots.
          • Keep the conversation going.                        • Visit CDC for specific recommendations.
          • Acknowledge benefits and risks.


          What If They Still Refuse to Vaccinate?
          • Excluding children from your practice: not recommended!
          • Teach about the clinical presentations of vaccine-preventable diseases.
          • Remind caregivers to call before bringing child to office visit if child is ill.
          • Alert other health care providers about which patients are not immunized.
          • Sign CDC’s refusal-to-vaccinate form.

Acute Symptomatic Seizure
• Provoked by an acute physiologic state
  $ Electrolyte abnormalities: hyponatremia, hypocalcemia
  $ Fever: “febrile seizures”

G6PD Deficiency                                                                                             NOTES
• G6PD is an enzyme that protects red blood cells.
• X-linked genetic disorder:
  $ May result in RBC damage or hemolysis
  $ May remain asymptomatic for life
     • Newborns with severe jaundice should be screened.
     • Screen if positive family history
     • Anemia varies depending on deficiency.
  $ Prevalent in tropical and subtropical zones: Africa, Europe, Asia
     • Kurdish Jewish (70%), Sardinian (4–35%), Nigerian (22%), Thai (17%), African American
        (12%), and more


G6PD Deficiency Presentation
• Asymptomatic
• Acute hemolytic anemia
  $ Triggered by “oxidative stress injury”
    • Illness/infection
    • Medications: fluoroquinolones, nitrofurantoin, phenazopyridine (Pyridium), sulfonylureas
       (glipizide, glyburide)
    • Chemical exposures/foods: fava beans, henna compounds, naphthalene (mothballs,
       lavatory deodorant); RUSH “poppers” (energy drinks)
    • Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole: probably safe


Sickle Cell Disease (SCD)
• Normal RBCs are biconcave discs.
• SCD RBCs are sickle-shaped.
  $ Inefficient carriers of O2 ➔ low O2 saturation
  $ Tend to clump together ➔ occlusion
• Clinical manifestations
  $ Hemolytic anemia and vaso-occlusion                                             Joshua Lanclos/APEA
     • Acute/chronic pain
     • Tissue ischemia/infarction
• Affects every body system

Leukemia
• Cancer of the bone marrow ➔ abnormal RBCs, WBCs, and/or platelets
• Most common form of cancer in children
• Two types of leukemia predominate in children
  $ Acute lymphocytic leukemia (ALL): 77%
  $ Acute myelogenous leukemia (AML): 11%
• Most common ages are 2–8 years
  $ Peak occurrence at 4 years
  $ Can occur at any age


Leukemia: Common Symptoms
• Anorexia, weight loss                                          • Persistent bone pain (21–38%)
• Fever                                                            $ Long bones: site of bone marrow!
• Hepatosplenomegaly (>60%)                                      • Lymphadenopathy (50%)
• Bleeding (50%)
  $ Platelet count less than 100,000

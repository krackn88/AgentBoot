          Dermatology: Terms to Know
          Abscess: deep infection, localized collection of pus
          Furuncle/boil: deep infection of hair follicles
          Bulla or blister: fluid-filled or pus-filled >0.5 cm
          Macule: flat change in skin with a color change (brown, blue, red, or hypopigmented)
          Nodule: solid lesion >0.5–2.0 cm (nodule >2.0 cm is a tumor)
          Papule: raised, solid lesion ≤0.5 cm; varies in color
          Plaque: raised, solid lesion >0.5 cm
          Vesicle: ≤0.5 cm elevated lesion that contains fluid
          Wheal (hive): transient rounded or flat-top plaque

Management of Bites                                                                                  NOTES
Clean and irrigate

Antibiotic prophylaxis
• Amoxicillin/clavulanate for 3 to 5 days if:
  $ Immunocompromised
  $ Suture closure
  $ Involvement of bones, joints, hands, face, or genitals
  $ Deep puncture wounds
  $ Human bites
• Tetanus prophylaxis if >5 years


Management of Bites
Rabies prophylaxis indications:
Animals:
  $ High-risk (skunks, raccoons, foxes, bats)
  $ Behaving atypically aggressive (squirrels, beavers)
  $ Cannot be obtained for testing
  $ Vaccination status unknown
• Refer emergently for:
  $ Rabies Immunoglobin (HRIG)
  $ Rabies Immunization (Rabivert): multidose series
• Reportable condition

Viral Rashes                                                                                            NOTES
• Viral exanthems: characteristic skin lesions associated with viruses (e.g., measles, rubella,
  varicella)
• Viral enanthems: oral eruption preceding, occurring simultaneously or following an exanthem
  (i.e., Koplik spots in measles)
• Other viruses with skin manifestations:
  $ Common viruses
      • HPV: common warts, plantar warts
      • Molluscum contagiosum: 2- to 5-mm umbilicated papules
      • Herpes simplex virus: two types (HSV1 and HSV2)
      • Herpes zoster: shingles
  $ Sexually transmitted
      • Human papilloma virus (HPV): genital warts
      • Genital herpes simplex
      • HIV: acute exanthem, infectious/neoplastic conditions

Herpes Zoster Management                                                                                     NOTES
• Very painful condition, prodrome
  $ NSAIDs: mild to moderate pain
  $ Narcotics: for severe pain
• Antiviral therapy:
  $ <72 hours since onset:
    • Oral acyclovir, famciclovir, valacyclovir, or brivudine for
       7 days
  $ >72 hours since onset:
    • New lesions are appearing (ongoing viral replication)
    • Immunocompromised




                     TIP: If patient is immunocompromised, ALWAYS prescribe
                           antiviral therapy regardless of time since onset.

• Two concepts:
  $ Potency: amount of drug needed to produce effect; affected by the amount of

          Skin Lesion Evaluation
          • Visual inspection:
            $ Asymmetry (one half does not match the other half)
            $ Border (irregular, not smooth)
            $ Color (more than one color in the same lesion)
            $ Diameter (more than 6 mm, pencil eraser)
            $ Evolving (changes in a mole)
          • Refer: lesions highly suspicious for malignancy
          • Punch/Shave biopsy: lesions of uncertain etiology, low suspicion for malignancy

            $ May be few or numerous lesions, cosmetic concerns
          • Management
            $ Symptomatic lesions
              • Cryotherapy
              • Curettage/shave excision
              • Electrodessication
            $ Biopsy suspicious lesions

Actinic (Solar) Keratosis                                                                                  NOTES
• Scaling macules, papules, or plaques in sun-exposed areas
  $ Precancerous
  $ May progress to squamous cell carcinoma; no way to
    predict if it will or it won’t
• Management
  $ Destructive therapies (liquid nitrogen cryosurgery; shave
    excision)
  $ Topicals (fluorouracil, imiquimod)
• Refer for Biopsy if:
  $ Indurated lesions
  $ Painful, ulcerated, or bleeding lesions
  $ Hyperkeratotic or hypertrophic actinic keratosis not
    resolving with standard therapy or recuring

Dermatologic Emergencies                                                                                NOTES
If you see these, refer ASAP!
• Erysipelas: well-demarcated bright red rash with raised edges in a toxic-looking patient; caused
    by Streptococcus pyogenes
• Necrotizing fasciitis: rare, rapidly progressing skin infection; red/purple skin, severe pain and
    fever, necrosis of affected area

                                  Erysipelas




Dermatologic Emergencies
Steven-Johnson Syndrome/Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis:
• Severe mucocutaneous reaction resulting in extensive necrosis and detachment of epidermis
   $ Caused by: medication or infection
• Prodrome: fever >102°F (38.9°C), flu-like symptoms 1 to 3 days
• Mucocutaneous lesions
   $ Target lesions with central dusky purpura
   $ Maybe bullae and blister formation
   $ Spreads from face downward
   $ May include positive Nikolsky sign (exfoliation of skin with rubbing)

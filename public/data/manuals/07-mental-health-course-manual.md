          Serotonin–Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitors (SNRIs)
          Side effects: nausea, dizziness, diaphoresis, increase in blood pressure (except duloxetine), sexual
          dysfunction, bleeding, hyponatremia
          Drug                        Indications/Considerations
          Duloxetine (Cymbalta)       MDD, GAD, fibromyalgia, neuropathy, chronic musculoskeletal pain; Least
                                      likely to raise BP

          Venlafaxine (Effexor)       MDD, social anxiety disorder, cataplexy; cautious use in patients at risk of
                                      bleeding

          Desvenlafaxine (Pristiq)    MDD; less risk of sexual dysfunction

          Milnacipran (Savella)       Fibromyalgia; may increase liver enzymes, avoid in end-stage renal disease

          Levomilnacipran (Fetzima)   MDD; avoid in end-stage renal disease


          Norepinephrine-Dopamine Reuptake Inhibitor (NDRI)
          • Drug: bupropion (Wellbutrin)
          • Indications
            $ MDD, seasonal affective disorder, smoking cessation (Zyban), ADHD (off-label)
            $ Bupropion/naltrexone (Contrave): weight loss
          • Contraindications
            $ Seizures, uncontrolled hypertension
          • Monitor: blood pressure
          • Common side effects: dry mouth, nausea, insomnia, anxiety
          • Benefits: no sexual side effects or weight gain; less likely to cause withdrawal syndrome


          Discontinuation Syndrome
          • Likely to occur if antidepressants are:
            $ Abruptly stopped
            $ Tapered too quickly (2–7 days)
            $ Switch to a new antidepressant with different pharmacokinetics
            $ Skips 3–4 consecutive doses
            $ Switch from branded to generic
          • Symptoms: fatigue, headache, nausea, agitation/anxiety, dizziness, insomnia, flulike illness (chills
            without fever, myalgias)

              TIP: Do NOT stop abruptly! Taper over 2–4 weeks for most SSRIs if taking SSRI for ≥6 weeks.

Methods for Switching Antidepressants                                                                      NOTES
Immediate switch
• Stop one and start the other.
• Use when drugs have similar MOA and half-lives.
Cross taper
• Taper current medication while increasing the new medication.
• Typically over 1–2 weeks
Washout period
• Taper current medication, allow a period of five half-lives to pass, and initiate new medication.
• Preferred if the two antidepressants cause significant drug–drug interactions (i.e., MAOI
  inhibitors)

Serotonin Syndrome
EXCESS SEROTONIN (SEROTONIN TOXICITY)
• Etiology
  $ Combining medications that increase serotonin levels (SSRIs, SNRIs, etc.)
• Symptoms
  $ Increased BP, HR, temp
  $ Confusion, restlessness, agitation
  $ Headache, muscle rigidity, N/V, tremors, shivering
  $ High rates of mortality
• Serotonin agents (impact serotonin in the body)
  $ Dextromethorphan
  $ Tramadol
  $ TCAs
  $ Cyclobenzaprine
  $ Others


Antidepressants and Suicidality

FDA black box warning reports are more prevalent:
• 18- to 24-year-olds
• Initial treatment
• First 1–2 months of treatment

NOTES     ADHD Management
          Preferred: CBT + pharmacologic treatment

          FIRST-LINE PHARMACOLOGIC:
          • Stimulants: amphetamines (Adderall), methylphenidate (Ritalin)
          • Side effects:
            $ Common: weight loss, headaches, insomnia, dry mouth, irritability, dysphoria
            $ Rare: priapism, elevated blood pressure, tics

          ALTERNATIVE TREATMENT OPTIONS:
          • Norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor: atomoxetine (Strattera); viloxazine (Qelbree)
          • Bupropion (Wellbutrin): off-label

          ADHD Management: Referral
          Refer when:
          • Comorbidities
            $ Psychological disorders (oppositional defiant disorder, emotional problems)
            $ Neurologic, medical disorders (tics, autism, sleep disorders)
          • Diagnosis is unclear.
          • Patient does not respond to treatment as expected.

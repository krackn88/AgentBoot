          Osteoarthritis
          • Joint pain: exacerbated by use
          • Stiffness: morning, ≤60 min
            $ Can affect any joint
               • May be unilateral or bilateral
               • Knees, hips, spine and hands
            $ Limited range of motion
            $ Joint tenderness
            $ Crepitus
            $ Joint effusion possible
            $ Bony remodeling


          Diagnosis of Osteoarthritis
          Clinical diagnosis
          • Joint pain (single/few)
          • Age ≥45 years
          • Morning stiffness (≤60 min)
          Radiograph supportive if:
          • Presence of joint space narrowing and/or
             osteophyte radiograph

                                                     QUESTION #9
                     A patient presents with their third acute gout flare within 12 months. In
                     addition to lifestyle modifications, the best management plan is to prescribe:
                        1. an anti-inflammatory agent only.
                        2. allopurinol only.
                        3. colchicine prophylactically.
                        4. allopurinol and an anti-inflammatory.




          Gout Treatment
          Acute flare: (based on patient characteristics)
          • Colchicine (avoid with renal/hepatic impairment)
          • NSAIDs (avoid with CVD, GI bleed, renal impairment)
          • Glucocorticoids (intra-articular, intramuscular, or oral)
          Prophylaxis: urate-lowering therapy
          • ≥2 flares annually
          • Intolerance to acute medications
          • Evidence of tophi or radiographic damage
          • Anti-inflammatory prophylaxis: 3–6 months after initiation of urate-lowering therapy
          • First-line: allopurinol

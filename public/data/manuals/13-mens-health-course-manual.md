NOTES     BPH: Management
          Alpha receptors are abundant in prostate, base of bladder, prostate capsule, and prostatic urethra.

          Alpha-adrenergic antagonists: decrease bladder outlet obstruction

           • Terazosin, tamsulosin, doxazosin

          5-alpha reductase inhibitors: reduce the size of the prostate

           • 6–12 months of treatment necessary to see full benefit
           • Dutasteride, finasteride

          PDE-5 inhibitor: relax bladder outlet and prostate smooth muscle

           • Tadalafil

          Combo (Jalyn) = dutasteride + tamsulosin


          Alpha Blockers Education and Precautions
          Caution with orthostatic hypotension
          Selective alpha blockers were originally used for blood pressure management.

          Uroselective                          Comments
          Alfuzosin/Uroxatral                   Alfuzosin (needs renal and hepatic dose adjustments)

          Tamsulosin/Flomax (generic)           Tamsulosin (no renal or hepatic precautions); sulfa allergy precaution

          Silodosin/Rapaflo                     Silodosin (needs renal and hepatic dose adjustments)

Prostate Cancer Assessment
• History
  $ Most patients are asymptomatic at diagnosis.
  $ May have new-onset ED and/or obstructive symptoms.
• Prostate-specific antigen (PSA)
  $ Sensitivity 21%; specificity 91%
  $ PSA >4 is suspicious.
  $ PSA value does not rule out/rule in prostate cancer.
• Digital rectal exam (DRE)
  $ Asymmetry, induration, nodularity are suspicious of prostate cancer even if normal PSA.

          Acute Bacterial Prostatitis (ABP)
          • Infection/inflammation of prostate
            $ Usually caused by ascending infection

          • Etiology

          • Acute pain: perineum, lower abdomen,
            testicles, or tip of the penis
          • Pain with ejaculation and/or defecation
          • Voiding difficulty: frequency, urgency, dysuria,
            nocturia
          • Acutely ill: fever, chills, malaise, myalgias

          Assessment

Management: Acute Bacterial Prostatitis
• Antibiotics for 2–6 weeks (empiric pending C&S)
  $ No STI risk:
    • Ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, or TMP-SMX
  $ STI risk: (gonorrhea/chlamydia)
    • 1g ceftriaxone (Rocephin) PLUS 2 weeks of doxycycline
• Expected course
  $ Symptoms improve in 2–6 days.
  $ Negative urinalysis at 7 days predicts cure IF patient completes full course of antibiotics.
• Defer PSA screening for 1 month after treatment.


Chronic Bacterial Prostatitis
• Cause: often untreated or undertreated
   acute prostatitis
• Symptoms: usually subtle, variable
   urologic pain, frequency, urgency
• Prostate exam is usually normal.
• Empiric antibiotics: 4–6 weeks
• Obtain urine culture.
• Possible NSAIDs, alpha blocker
Maintain a LOW threshold for UROLOGY
referral!!!​

                                       EPIDIDYMITIS

Epididymitis: Etiology
• Noninfectious causes: most common
  $ Reflux of urine through the
     ejaculatory ducts and vas deferens
     into epididymis
  $ Prolonged sitting
  $ Underlying congenital defect
• Infectious: rare unless STI
  $ 75% of time: C. trachomatis or
     N. gonorrhoeae
  $ C. trachomatis most common


Epididymitis: Clinical
Presentation
• Gradual development of scrotal pain
• Dysuria
• Physical exam
  $ Epididymis very tender, enlarged,                                            Joshua Lanclos/APEA
    and indurated
  $ May have urethral discharge if gonorrhea
  $ Positive Prehn sign: relief of pain with scrotal elevation


Epididymitis: Diagnostic Studies
• Urinalysis + culture
  $ Normal in noninfectious epididymitis
  $ Pyuria: think infectious!
• NAAT for C. trachomatis or N. gonorrhoeae
• Ultrasound if no improvement or an uncertain
  diagnosis


Epididymitis: Management
• Noninfectious, no severe pain
   $ Treatment is conservative: rest, oral fluids,
      scrotal support, ice.
   $ NSAIDs/analgesics
• Infectious (based on culture)
   $ Gonorrhea: ceftriaxone 500 mg IM
   $ Chlamydia: doxycycline 100 mg BID ×10 days
Expect improvement within 3 days.
Full resolution within 2–4 weeks of treatment

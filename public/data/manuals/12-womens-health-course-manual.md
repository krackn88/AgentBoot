Primary Dysmenorrhea Management                                                                             NOTES
• Nonpharmacologic: heat and exercise
• Pharmacologic:
  $ NSAIDs: initiated at onset (or a few days prior) to menses
  $ Hormonal contraceptives
    • Combined hormonal contraceptives: pill, patch, ring
    • Progestin injections: medroxyprogesterone acetate (Depo-Provera) (q3 months)
    • Progestin IUDs: Mirena and Liletta (8 years), Kyleena (5 years), and Skyla (3 years);
       all release levonorgestrel
• No relief? Not following expected course?
  $ Look for secondary cause and refer!

Cervical Cancer                                                        Ubiquitous!!!
Caused by the human papillomavirus (HPV)
• HPV usually clears within 8–24 months
  (especially if ≤24 years old).
  $ Persistent infection—causes cancer
• High-risk (hrHPV) subtypes
  $ Types 16, 18: 70% HPV-related cancers
  $ 12 other known hrHPV subtypes
  $ Other cancers:
     • Oropharynx

     • Penis
     • Anal
     • Cervix
     • Vagina, vulva


HPV Vaccination: Gardasil
• Covers nine of the HPV subtypes (including two strains that commonly cause genital warts!)
Immunization schedule:
Ages 9–14:
  $ 2 doses, given 6–12 months apart
Ages 15–26 or if immunocompromised:
  $ 3 doses, given at 0, 1–2, 6 months
Ages 27–45:
  $ shared decision making


Cervical Screening Average Risk Patients
Average risk
• Asymptomatic
• Immunocompetent
• Hx of normal screening results (if previously screened)

Perimenopause
Body’s transition to menopause:
• Mean length 4 years​; median duration 7–10 years!
• Starts at different ages (as early as mid-30s!)​
• Low estrogen states: hormonal testing not useful
Symptoms                                            Genitourinary symptoms
• Irregular menses                                  • Vaginal dryness
• Cognitive changes (“Brain Fog”)                   • Dyspareunia
• Sleep/mood changes                                • Urinary urgency
• Hot flashes and night sweats                      • Urinary frequency
• Osteoporosis risk                                 • Recurrent UTIs
• Cardiovascular risk


Menopausal Management
Hormone replacement therapy (first-line)
• COC: perimenopause and desire contraception
• Estrogen only: total hysterectomy
• Estrogen + Progestin: if uterus present
• Contraindications: same as CHC
Nonhormonal therapy (useful for contraindications/patient wishes)
• FDA approved:
  $ Paroxetine (Brisdelle) 7.5 mg daily
  $ Fezolinetant 45 mg daily
• Off-label: Supported by the North American Menopause Society
  $ SSRIs: citalopram, escitalopram
  $ SNRIs: desvenlafaxine, venlafaxine
  $ Gabapentinoids: gabapentin


         TIP: Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest amount of time for symptoms.

Bone Health                                                                                       NOTES
• Calcium
  $ Ensure sufficient intake (≥1,200 mg daily)
• Vitamin D
  $ Ensure sufficient intake (800–1,000 IU daily)
  $ Supplement with vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol)
• Modifiable risk factors for osteoporosis
  $ Reduce smoking, alcohol, medications/oral steroids
  $ Weight-bearing, balance, and resistance exercises
  $ Fall prevention


Osteoporosis Screening
Risk factors
• Advanced age
• Previous fragility fracture
• Low body weight
• Parental history of hip fracture
• Rheumatoid arthritis
• Cigarette smoking
• Excess alcohol intake
• Early menopause
• Long-term glucocorticoid therapy

Risk factor assessment (FRAX tool)
• 10-year probability of osteoporotic fracture


Screening: DEXA scan (Gold standard)
• Females ≥65 years old
• Females/males at any age with risk factors


Osteoporosis Diagnosis & Management

Osteopenia            T-Score −1.0 to −2.5

Osteoporosis          T-Score <−2.5

Who needs pharmacologic therapy?
1. Osteopenia + increased FRAX score
2. Osteopenia + Hx of fragility fracture
3. Osteoporosis


First-line: bisphosphonates
• Alendronate (Fosamax)
• Risedronate (Actonel, Atelvia)
• Ibandronate (Boniva)
• Zoledronic acid (Reclast)

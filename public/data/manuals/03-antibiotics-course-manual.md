NOTES     How Common Is Allergy to Both Penicillin and Cephalosporins?
          • IgE-mediated cross-reactivity is very uncommon!
          • Strongest possibility if similar R1 chemical side chain
          • 1–8%: first/second generation cephalosporins
          • <1%: third-generation cephalosporins
          Penicillin                                                Cephalosporin




                        PRESCRIBING RULES FOR PENICILLIN ALLERGY

          Rule 1: NEVER Give a First-Generation Cephalosporin if
          The reaction to penicillin was anaphylaxis or hives!
          Immunoglobulin E (IgE)-mediated, type I hypersensitivity reaction: Each reaction progressively
          worsens.




          Other IgE-mediated symptoms: hypotension, laryngeal edema, wheezing, angioedema, or
          urticaria (hives)


          Rule 2: Cautious Use of Second- or Third-Generation Cephalosporin if
          • Type 1 hypersensitivity to PCN
          • Later generations have dissimilar chemical side chains. ➔ Less potential for cross-reactivity
          • Less potential for cross-reactivity if >10 years since allergic reaction to penicillin

                                       TIP: <1% of the population has a true PCN allergy.
